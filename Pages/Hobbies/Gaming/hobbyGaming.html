Why I play

The honest answer is that it's the easiest way I have to spend time with people who don't live nearby any more. We're spread across different schedules and different parts of the island, and a lobby is the one place we all reliably turn up.

The games themselves matter less than they look like they do. Some nights it's ranked and everyone's locked in; some nights it's whatever went free that week and nobody's really playing at all. Both count.

Nobody remembers the rank. Everyone remembers the call at 2am when the whole team was crying laughing over a lost round.

It goes where I go

The best thing about gaming isn't any one game — it's that it bends around the day. Twenty minutes on a train, an hour winding down, or a whole evening with the door shut and the headset on.

  1. On the go

    A phone or a handheld turns dead time into something. The commute, the gap between classes, the ten minutes before a meeting starts.

    Mostly whatever's free that week. Low stakes by design.

  2. Winding down

    Low stakes, low volume, lights off. Something slow enough to put down mid-sentence, and forgiving enough that playing badly costs nothing.

    Roblox filled this slot for years. Now it's casual Brawlhalla.

  3. Locked in at the desk

    The other end of it: proper setup, full attention, a difficulty that asks something of you. Ranked Valorant or Counter-Strike, comms on.

    This is the version that got me to the top 1–2%.

  4. And it's never really solo

    Friends in different places, same Discord call, most weeks. The game is half the reason we still talk regularly.

    Same four or five people, most nights, for years now.
