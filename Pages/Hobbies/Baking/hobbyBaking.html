About The Hobby

Baking rewards patience in a way few hobbies do — you can't rush a proof or open the oven early without paying for it later. What starts as flour, butter, and sugar on a counter turns into something you can actually share, which is half the reason it's worth doing at all.

The Craft

From Flour to Finished Bake

Every bake follows roughly the same rhythm. Here's how a batch comes together, start to finish.

Mise en Place

Weigh every ingredient before anything touches a bowl. Baking is chemistry, not guesswork.

Mix & Knead

Bring the dough or batter together until the structure is exactly where it needs to be.

Proof & Rest

Time does half the work here — flavour and texture develop while you wait it out.

Bake

Heat transforms everything. This is where the smell takes over the whole kitchen.

Cool & Finish

Glazing, frosting, or just resisting the urge to cut in before it's set.

Personal Growth

Benefits of Baking

What kneading dough at 9pm is actually doing for you, beyond the smell.

A Reason to Slow Down

Kneading, proofing, and waiting for a timer force a pace that's hard to find anywhere else in the day.

Precision Under Pressure

Off ratios and mistimed bakes teach measurement discipline fast — there's no eyeballing your way out of a collapsed cake.

Edible Creativity

Flavour pairings, piping, and plating give you a genuine creative outlet — one you get to eat afterwards.

Baker's Bucket List

5 Bakes Every Baker Should Master

The recipes that teach you the most, in roughly the order to attempt them.

1. Chocolate Chip Cookies

The forgiving starting point — teaches you how butter temperature changes spread and chew.

2. Vanilla Layer Cake

Where levelling, stacking, and crumb coats stop being optional and start being technique.

3. Sourdough Loaf

No shortcuts here — you're managing a living culture over days, not hours.

4. Butter Croissants

Lamination is unforgiving — this is the one that separates hobbyists from the patient.

5. French Macarons

The final boss. Humidity, folding, and oven temperature all have to agree at once.
